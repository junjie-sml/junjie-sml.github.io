About / Research

My research seeks a mechanistic understanding of how molecular programs, cells, and tissues organize and communicate. I pursue this through interpretable statistical modeling and graph-based machine learning across single-cell and spatial omics.

1. Graphical models and gene regulatory inference in scRNA-seq data

Inferring gene regulatory networks (GRNs) from single-cell RNA-seq is central to explaining how cells maintain identity, transition between states, and respond to perturbations. Unlike bulk assays, scRNA-seq yields count-valued, sparse, and heterogeneous measurements at cellular resolution, making it essential to separate direct (conditional-dependence) relationships from correlations driven by confounding, technical noise, or compositional mixing. My work develops graphical models explicitly tailored to count gene expression, using Poisson log-normal (PLN) and its mixture extension (MPLN) to link latent continuous structure with observed counts and thereby infer GRNs in a statistically principled way. In homogeneous populations, PLN-based estimators recover precision-matrix structure that reflects putative regulatory dependencies with high-dimensional robustness. In mixed populations—where cell types or states co-occur and labels may be uncertain—the MPLN formulation preserves identifiability and enables joint estimation of cell-type–specific GRNs. Together, these frameworks provide scalable, interpretable network inference aligned with the data-generating process of scRNA-seq, offering a reliable foundation for mechanism-driven biology.

2. Super-resolution interpretable embedding learning for spatial transcriptomics

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) technologies have transformed the study of tissue organization by capturing gene expression within spatial context, yet current data remain limited by coarse spatial resolution, noise, and sparsity. These constraints make it difficult to uncover fine-grained tissue architectures and the molecular programs embedded within them. My research addresses this challenge by developing interpretable representation learning frameworks that integrate spatial gene expression with high-resolution histology. By embedding non-negative, parts-based factorization within multimodal encoder–decoder architectures, these models learn biologically meaningful, high-definition embeddings that refine spatial resolution while preserving interpretability. Beyond improving visualization, this approach provides a transparent, quantitative map of how gene expression, histological features, and spatial organization jointly define tissue structure and function across samples and technologies.

3. Intercellular functional interaction module inference for spatial transcriptomics

Cell–cell interaction shapes tissue organization, function, and homeostasis. Spatial transcriptomics now enables joint measurement of gene expression and cellular positions, opening the door to studying intercellular communication in situ. Yet a key gap remains: individual ligand–receptor contacts do not readily explain how coordinated, directional programs of communication emerge to drive tissue dynamics. My research addresses this by modeling intercellular signaling as structured, pathway-aligned modules. Framed as a latent representation problem, spatial gene expression is embedded into a space of directional meta-interactions (MIs) that link upstream sender regulators, ligand–receptor bridges, and downstream receiver targets at cell–cell resolution. This formulation yields compact, interpretable bases of communication that jointly account for cell–cell interactions and cellular gene expression, enabling robust identification of regulatory programs, multi-hop MI cascades, and phenotype-predictive signals across diverse datasets. Applications to CosMx ovarian cancer and Perturb-FISH melanoma highlight THBS-mediated CAF–tumor signaling coupled to a monocyte–fibroblast–tumor SPP1→THBS cascade, as well as NF-κB-coupled TGF-β pathways in melanoma–T-cell crosstalk. By unifying mechanistic interpretability with predictive utility, this agenda provides a coherent framework to resolve, compare, and perturb multicellular communication underlying tissue states and disease progression.
